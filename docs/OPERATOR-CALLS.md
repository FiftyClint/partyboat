# Operator calls: the guide for Ryan

The single most important question in this whole test: **will a local party boat
operator pay a flat fee for trip requests?** The sites can be perfect and it means
nothing if the answer is no. These calls are how we find out.

Prospect lists, with each business's public phone number and what they offer, are in
`docs/research/operators-<market>.csv`. Every entry was checked on the business's own
website on the date shown. Call the business number listed, never a personal cell.

## Before the first call

- Work the list in the order given in each market's summary. The top three per market
  are marked with why.
- Have the GHL `Operator Partners` pipeline open. Log every call, including no answer.
- Calls only. No cold texts. Email only to an address the business publishes, and only
  to follow up a conversation.

## What we can and cannot say

Say:
- We publish independent local guide sites for party boats, one per lake.
- We own the site and the phone number, and we send each market's requests to one
  operator only.
- They pay a flat monthly or seasonal fee. We take nothing from their bookings.
- They will see every request we get, so they can judge it for themselves.
- The sites are new. We do not have traffic numbers yet.

Never say:
- Any number of leads, calls or visitors. We have none yet. Inventing one ends trust the
  moment they check.
- That we rank on Google, or will by a date.
- That anyone else in their market has signed.
- Anything about their competitors that is not on the competitor's own website.

## The opener

> Hi, this is Ryan. I'm calling about party boats on [lake]. We publish an independent
> guide site for [lake] and we're picking one operator there to send our trip requests
> to, exclusively. Before we choose, I want to understand how your season works. Do you
> have five minutes, or is there a better time?

If they ask "how many leads do you get?": "We're new and we don't have numbers to show
you yet. That's exactly why I'm asking what it would need to be worth to you. When
we do have requests, you'll see every one."

## The questions, in this order

Write the answers in the GHL contact note. Exact words where you can.

1. What do you run: captained charters, self-drive pontoons, party barges? How many
   boats, and the largest group you can take?
2. Which dates or days go unsold in a normal season? (Weekdays? Shoulder months?)
3. How far ahead do people book your summer weekends?
4. Where do your customers come from now? Do you pay Boatsetter, GetMyBoat, Viator or
   anyone else per booking? Roughly what does it cost you?
5. Who answers your phone on a Saturday at 9pm? How fast do you call back a web request?
6. If we sent you 10 real trip requests a month in season, all for your lake, would a
   flat fee be worth it to you? Then ask about 20 and 30.
7. What would you pay a month for that, in season? Off season? Get a number or a range.
   "It depends" is fine; ask on what.
8. Would you rather pay per request than flat? Which would you actually sign?
9. Can you show us your current captain's license and insurance certificate before we
   start sending requests? (If no, they are not a fit. Be polite and move on.)
10. Can we follow up by email? Which address?

## After each call

- Move the contact to the right stage: Talked, Interested, or Not now.
- If they gave a price or range, put it in the note as `PRICE: $___ / month, season ___`.
- Anything that sounds like an unsafe or unlicensed operation (no captain's license,
  more passengers than the boat allows), note it and mark Not now. Do not argue.

## What counts as a result

From the plan, in order of strength:
hypothetical interest < a specific price they would pay < a signed conditional pilot <
a paid invoice < a second payment.

The goal for this round: **a specific monthly or seasonal price from at least two
operators in at least two markets.** That is what decides whether the test continues.
